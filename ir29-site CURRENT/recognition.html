Unit Recognition Program

Verdienst im Verein

Real awards members can earn through participation — kept intentionally separate from the historical decorations on the Awards & Decorations page.

I.R. 29Verein
Why this is separate from Awards & Decorations: the Eisernes Kreuz and other WWI decorations belong to a persona's historical portrayal — they represent what a real soldier plausibly earned in 1914–18, and we don't treat them as something a reenactor "unlocks" through modern event attendance. The recognitions below are the opposite: they're honestly modern, awarded for real things members do today, and are not worn as part of the historical impression at events. Think unit challenge coin or certificate, not uniform decoration.

Service Awards

  • Bronze Service Award — 5 events attended
  • Silver Service Award — 10 events attended
  • Gold Service Award — 20 events attended

Research Commendation

  • Awarded for a confirmed, sourced contribution to the unit's historical research — e.g., resolving an open item like the regiment's company-level Somme/Flanders assignments or Sturmkompanie history
  • Must cite a real primary or reputable secondary source

Kit Excellence Award

  • Judged informally at an event by unit leadership (or a neutral outside reenactor, if available) for the most accurate, well-assembled impression
  • Not a substitute for basic kit standards — this recognizes members who go beyond them

Mentor's Ribbon

  • Awarded to a member who has helped mentor new recruits (Rekrut/Musketier) through building their first complete impression
  • Recognizes teaching and patience, not seniority alone

Founder's Commendation

  • A discretionary award from the Gruppenleiter for outstanding contribution to the unit that doesn't fit the other categories
  • Intended to be rare, not routine
How these are presented: as a small unit pin, ribbon, or printed certificate — worn on civilian clothing or displayed off the historical impression, never pinned to a period tunic alongside real WWI decorations. This keeps the line between "what a 1916–18 soldier earned" and "what a 2026 member earned" clean for anyone looking at the unit, including the public at living-history displays.
Suggestions welcome
This program will evolve as the unit grows — if you have ideas for additional recognitions, bring them up in Discord or reach out via the Join page.
